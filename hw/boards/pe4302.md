# PE4302 digital step attenuator module

Photo: [photos/pe4302-module.jpg](../photos/pe4302-module.jpg). The module has **parallel control only**: a 7-pin header with `GND V1 V2 V3 V4 V5 V6`. There is no serial interface (the Silicon Chip description of this module says it is hard-wired for parallel programming), so no SPI.

## What the photo shows
- SMA `RFin` on one end and `RFout` on the other, silkscreen `PE4302`.
- Header on the right edge, silkscreen from the top: `GND V1 V2 V3 V4 V5 V6` (V6 is the pin nearest `RFout`).
- Two solder pads to the left of the chip with a red and a black lead soldered on: this is the supply (VDD, GND). The supply is **not** on the header.
- A small series part in each RF trace next to the SMA connectors (looks like DC-blocking capacitors); confirm with a DMM (no DC path from the SMA centre pin to the chip).
- No voltage regulator on the board.

## Facts (module description and PE4302 datasheet)

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Part | PE4302 (pSemi / Peregrine), 6-bit, 0.5 dB steps, 31.5 dB, DC to 4 GHz, about 1.5 dB insertion loss |
| Supply | **3.3 V**: the Silicon Chip description says the chip must be connected to 3.3 V and powers the module from a microcontroller 3.3 V pin. The chip itself allows 2.7 to 5.5 V |
| Inputs | V1 = 0.5 dB, V2 = 1 dB, V3 = 2 dB, V4 = 4 dB, V5 = 8 dB, V6 = 16 dB; **high = stage switched in** |
| Pull resistors | all V1 to V6 inputs have **10 kohm pull-downs**: open inputs = 0 dB attenuation |
| V6 | reaches the chip's 16 dB pin through a series 10 kohm resistor (check that 3.3 V on V6 really switches the 16 dB stage, see below) |
| Serial pins | LE tied high, P/S and PUP pins tied low: "parallel immediate mode", hard-wired |
| Logic threshold | a high input needs 0.7 x VDD, so at VDD = 3.3 V the SBC's 3.3 V GPIO is fine; with a 5 V supply it would not be |
| Switching | about 1 us to settle (datasheet); the GPIO writes are the slow part |
| RF ports | must be at 0 V DC or DC-blocked |

## Control from the SBC: through a PCF8574 on I2C (since 2026-11-25)

The six inputs hang on a PCF8574 port expander; no SBC pin drives them. The expander is on an I2C-to-LCD adapter board ("LCD backpack"), used without a display.

| PE4302 input | PCF8574 port |
|---|---|
| V1 (0.5 dB) | P0 |
| V2 (1 dB) | P1 |
| V3 (2 dB) | P2 |
| none (there is no V0 on this module) | P3: LED backlight of the adapter board, `[attenuator] pcf8574_backlight` |
| V4 (4 dB) | P4 |
| V5 (8 dB) | P5 |
| V6 (16 dB) | P6 |
| none | P7: written high |

- **Address:** `[attenuator] pcf8574_address`, default 0x27 (PCF8574 with A0..A2 open). A PCF8574A answers at 0x38..0x3F (0x3F with A0..A2 open). `i2cdetect` shows which it is.
- **Bus:** the BBB's I2C2 (P9.19 SCL, P9.20 SDA), or on the EBAZ4205 DATA2-20 (SCL) and DATA2-11 (SDA), `/dev/i2c-0`. 100 kHz maximum (the PCF8574's limit). While the EBAZ's PL engine owns the pins it also owns this bus.
- **Supply the expander board from 3.3 V.** It usually carries its own I2C pull-ups to its VCC: from 5 V they would pull SCL and SDA to 5 V, which neither SBC tolerates.
- **Pull-ups on V1..V6 are required, not optional.** A PCF8574 output drives low strongly but high only with about 100 uA. Against the module's 10 kohm pull-down that gives about 1 V, below the PE4302's 2.3 V threshold. With 2.2 kohm to 3.3 V on each line the high level is about 2.7 V, and the expander sinks about 1.5 mA per low line. The adapter board's R27..R32 are these pull-ups, on the module side of its 220 ohm resistors: wiring P0..P2 and P4..P6 to pins 2..7 of its PE4302 header J4 uses them. Then leave the BBB's P8.7..P8.12 as inputs: the board still connects them to the same lines. Check with a DMM: V high must be above 2.3 V.
- **Power-up:** the PCF8574 starts with all ports high, so with the pull-ups the attenuator starts at **31.5 dB**. Without power on the expander, but with the pull-ups powered, check what the lines do before relying on it. Open inputs are still 0 dB (the module's pull-downs).
- Driver: `src/sadiy/drivers/pe4302.py` (`PE4302Pcf8574`) over `drivers/pcf8574.py`. At start all stages are switched in. All six lines change with one byte; a change that switches stages both in and out is two bytes in one transaction: first the new stages in, then the old ones out, so no intermediate state is less attenuating than the lower of the old and new setting.
- **Read-back:** bench mode and the walk tool read the port back after writing. A difference means a missing pull-up (a line written high that reads low) or a short.
- Check V6: drive it high with 3.3 V and measure the voltage at the chip's 16 dB pin. If a 10 kohm series resistor and a pull-down on the chip side form a divider (about 1.65 V), the 16 dB stage will not switch in. The walk tool shows it as no 16 dB step. Fix by bridging the series resistor.
- Identify and confirm the lines: `python tools/bringup.py pe4302-walk` (`--platform ebaz` before the command on the EBAZ4205; stop `sweepd` first) switches one stage at a time. It was written for a tone through the attenuator into the log detector (each stage raises the output by weight x 24.5 mV). The detector is dead: measure the attenuated tone with the scope instead, or after the mixer.
- Preview any change: `python tools/bringup.py pe4302-pattern --db 16 --from-db 15.5`.

## Still to check on the board
- Voltage actually applied to the supply pads. The red and black leads go toward the shared lever connector in the overview photo: confirm 3.3 V with a DMM before the SBC is connected. The chip's absolute maximum is far below a 9 to 12 V rail.
- Series parts in the RF traces are capacitors (DC blocks).
- Which header end is `GND` (silkscreen says the end nearest `RFin`).
