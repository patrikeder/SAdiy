# PE4302 digital step attenuator module

Photo: [photos/pe4302-module.jpg](../photos/pe4302-module.jpg). The module has **parallel control only**: a 7-pin header with `GND V1 V2 V3 V4 V5 V6`. There is no serial interface (the Silicon Chip description of this module says it is hard-wired for parallel programming), so no SPI.

## What the photo shows
- SMA `RFin` on one end and `RFout` on the other, silkscreen `PE4302`.
- Header on the right edge, silkscreen from the top: `GND V1 V2 V3 V4 V5 V6` (V6 is the pin nearest `RFout`).
- Two solder pads to the left of the chip with a red and a black lead soldered on: this is the supply (VDD, GND). The supply is **not** on the header.
- A small series part in each RF trace next to the SMA connectors (looks like DC-blocking capacitors); confirm with a DMM (no DC path from the SMA centre pin to the chip).
- No voltage regulator on the board.

## Facts (module description and PE4302 datasheet)

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Part | PE4302 (pSemi / Peregrine), 6-bit, 0.5 dB steps, 31.5 dB, DC to 4 GHz, about 1.5 dB insertion loss |
| Supply | **3.3 V**: the Silicon Chip description says the chip must be connected to 3.3 V and powers the module from a microcontroller 3.3 V pin. The chip itself allows 2.7 to 5.5 V |
| Inputs | V1 = 0.5 dB, V2 = 1 dB, V3 = 2 dB, V4 = 4 dB, V5 = 8 dB, V6 = 16 dB; **high = stage switched in** |
| Pull resistors | all V1 to V6 inputs have **10 kohm pull-downs**: open inputs = 0 dB attenuation |
| V6 | reaches the chip's 16 dB pin through a series 10 kohm resistor (check that 3.3 V on V6 really switches the 16 dB stage, see below) |
| Serial pins | LE tied high, P/S and PUP pins tied low: "parallel immediate mode", hard-wired |
| Logic threshold | a high input needs 0.7 x VDD, so at VDD = 3.3 V the SBC's 3.3 V GPIO is fine; with a 5 V supply it would not be |
| Switching | about 1 us to settle (datasheet); the GPIO writes are the slow part |
| RF ports | must be at 0 V DC or DC-blocked |

## Control from the SBC
- Six GPIO lines per SBC (`../wiring-bbb.md`, `../wiring-rpi.md`) plus a common ground to the `GND` header pin.
- Driver: `src/sadiy/drivers/pe4302.py`. At start all stages are switched in (31.5 dB). Stages turning on are written before stages turning off, so no intermediate state is less attenuating than the lower of the old and new setting.
- **Open inputs are 0 dB** (the module's pull-downs). While the SBC is off or booting the attenuator passes the full signal. If that matters, add 2.2 kohm pull-ups to 3.3 V on V1 to V6: with the 10 kohm pull-down that gives about 2.7 V at the input, above the 2.3 V threshold, so the default becomes 31.5 dB. The SBC pins then sink about 1.5 mA each when driving low.
- Check V6: drive it high with 3.3 V and measure the voltage at the chip's 16 dB pin. If a 10 kohm series resistor and a pull-down on the chip side form a divider (about 1.65 V), the 16 dB stage will not switch in. The walk tool shows it as no 16 dB step. Fix by bridging the series resistor.
- Identify and confirm the lines: `python tools/bringup.py pe4302-walk --gpio ...` with a low-level tone through the attenuator and the detector. Each stage switched in should raise the detector output by weight x 24.5 mV.
- Preview any change: `python tools/bringup.py pe4302-pattern --db 16 --from-db 15.5`.

## Still to check on the board
- Voltage actually applied to the supply pads. The red and black leads go toward the shared lever connector in the overview photo: confirm 3.3 V with a DMM before the SBC is connected. The chip's absolute maximum is far below a 9 to 12 V rail.
- Series parts in the RF traces are capacitors (DC blocks).
- Which header end is `GND` (silkscreen says the end nearest `RFin`).
